# Investigation 1 — heartbeats-sleep

## NOTE on data pivot (decided after AnAge inspection)

AnAge build-14 does not contain heart rate. It does contain **metabolic rate (W)** for 517 mammals+birds with mass and lifespan. Heart rate scales tightly with metabolic rate via Kleiber's law (HR ∝ MR^(1/4)), so the underlying claim is unchanged but better-tested with MR directly. The investigation name is kept for continuity; the actual analysis uses **lifetime metabolic budget** (J/lifetime) instead of beats/lifetime.

## Central claim

After phylogenetic correction, **lifetime sleep hours predicts cross-species `log(MR × lifespan)` better than mass alone**. This is a direct test of Rubner's rate-of-living hypothesis: does cumulative time in low-metabolic state (sleep) explain the residuals of lifetime energy expenditure beyond what mass predicts?

`lifetime_energy ≈ MR (W) × seconds_per_year × max_lifespan (yr)`

## Pre-registered nested-model comparison

Predictor sets in PGLS regression on `log(beats_per_lifetime)`:

- **M0 (null):** intercept only
- **M1 (mass):** `log(M_kg)` + class
- **M2 (mass + sleep):** `log(M_kg)` + class + `log(sleep_hrs/day)`
- **M3 (mass + metabolic proxy):** `log(M_kg)` + class + `log(activity_hrs/day)` where `activity_hrs = 24 − sleep_hrs`

If sleep is the load-bearing predictor (not just a proxy for activity), M2 should beat M3 by AIC.

## Decision rule

- **GRADUATE to paper** if BOTH:
  1. PGLS coefficient on `log(sleep_hrs/day)` in M2 has p < 0.05
  2. AIC(M2) − AIC(M1) ≤ −4

- **KILL** if:
  - sleep coefficient is not significant (p ≥ 0.05) AND AIC drop < 2
  - OR merged species table falls below N = 25

## Sample size & power note

PanTHERIA gives sleep for ~600 mammals; AnAge has heart rate + lifespan for ~150 mammals + ~80 birds. Realistic intersection: ~40–60 mammals + ~5–10 birds. PGLS at 3 predictors on N=50 has ~80% power to detect a moderate effect (Cohen's f² ≈ 0.15).

## Reportable secondary findings even if KILL

- Coefficient and CI on sleep (whether or not significant)
- Phylogenetic signal (Pagel's λ) on residuals
- Bird-vs-mammal AIC comparison separately (cross-class might be too heterogeneous)
- Outliers (likely: hummingbirds, bats, naked mole-rats)

## Why this might surprise an expert

The aging literature dominantly attributes the heartbeats rule failure in birds to mitochondrial uncoupling / lower oxidative damage. Sleep as the explanatory covariate is *under-tested* — Siegel 2005 noted the sleep correlation in passing but never controlled it against the metabolic-rate hypothesis with PGLS.
